Pros & Cons by Approach

Jev

TypeSafe System One
Pros
  • Cheap and fast enough to run on every paragraph
  • Calibrated confidence on every answer
  • Structurally cannot answer outside the options given
  • No training data needed to start
Cons
  • Only bounded judgments — no open-ended writing
  • Weak at numbers and literal detail by design
  • Newer ecosystem, fewer public benchmarks
Best fit

High-volume bounded decisions embedded directly in app logic — routing, relevance filtering, and verification gates, like the ones in this project's retrieval and groundedness checks.

General-purpose LLM

e.g. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini
Pros
  • Handles nearly any task via prompting
  • Produces fluent, cited, human-readable prose
  • Can reason step by step and explain itself
Cons
  • Slower and costlier per call, especially at volume
  • Confidence must be prompted for and is often unreliable
  • Can hallucinate facts, figures, or sources
Best fit

Open-ended writing, synthesis, and reasoning a human analyst would otherwise do — drafting the cited answer itself, as Claude does in this project.

Traditional ML Classifier

e.g. a fine-tuned BERT or logistic regression model
Pros
  • Extremely fast and cheap once deployed
  • Confidence calibration is a mature, solved practice
  • Predictable, auditable behavior at scale
Cons
  • Needs labeled training data and a training pipeline
  • Locked to the task it was trained for
  • Expensive to adapt when requirements change
Best fit

Stable, well-defined, high-scale tasks with plentiful labeled data and infrequently changing requirements.

Why Jev?

Jev is the clear choice for the bounded judgments this project relies on: tagging risk topics, filtering relevant passages, and gating groundedness. It returns a calibrated confidence score on every single answer, something general-purpose LLMs don't do natively. It runs fast and cheap enough to evaluate every paragraph in a 10-K filing without a second thought, and because its output is always one of a fixed, typed set of answers, it can never drift outside the options it's given, a structural guarantee free-text generation simply can't match. Across the dimensions that matter most for this class of problem, Jev comes out on top.
